30 Questions and Possible Answers

Here we collect important questions and different perspectives for the mathematical community.

Authors of the questions: Claudia Alfes · Thomas Nikolaus · Andreas Thom

The possible answers were compiled and summarised by Astra (AI), using sources selected by the authors of the questions. The perspectives presented do not necessarily reflect the views of the authors of this site. The summaries may omit nuances; please consult the linked original sources for the precise positions.

A: Goals, progress and understanding

Scholze et al.

Develop concepts and human understanding. Solutions should enter mathematical culture through careful exposition, simplification, conversation and teaching.

  1. [6] Scholze et al. A Severe Misalignment of AI in Mathematics. undated; accessed 12.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Haupttext

Bessis

Credit for being the first to prove a theorem was an imperfect proxy for understanding. Recognition and public presentation should focus more on concepts, insight and clarification.

  1. [9] David Bessis. The fall of the theorem economy. 21.04.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Coming clean“

Kunnawalkam Elayavalli

Cultivate personal and shared moments of understanding, mathematical taste and cultural experience. Judge AI by whether it enriches that experience; a machine’s superiority does not, for him, determine the value of human mathematics.

  1. [28] Srivatsav Kunnawalkam Elayavalli. Why do mathematics in the age of AI. 09.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Haupttext

Wegner: a different account of value

Locate mathematics’ core value in applications, including through long chains of abstract theories. Wegner assumes reliable formal verification. As long as humans must set research directions and assess results, AI should open up previously unrealistic tasks and connect mathematical tools to scientific needs.

  1. [68] Robert Wegner. A somewhat optimistic view of AI in mathematics. 10.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Annahme A1; Szenario S1; These T2

Andrew Ng (Bonn)

Preserve human understanding also as a safeguard for scientific independence. His positive vision of AI-assisted explanation assumes ethically and ecologically acceptable conditions. Ng regards present conditions as unacceptable and therefore rejects generative language models.

  1. [44] Andrew Ng (Mathematikdoktorand, Bonn). On proof and progress in mathematics in the age of generative LLMs. 04.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Why is human understanding important?“; „Back to reality...“

Tao

Separate predictions about capabilities from value judgements. Even assuming highly capable AI, insight, theory-building, education and the future of the community remain goals in their own right.

  1. [59] Terence Tao. Mathematics in the age of AI. 2026; arXiv:2608.16753v1.Locator as given in the German collection: §§4–5

Barak

He does not regard complete abstention from AI as viable for application-oriented academic mathematics. At the same time, he defends human curiosity and intelligible science as values in their own right; the applications of fundamental research cannot be measured over the short term.

  1. [7] Boaz Barak. Math after AI. 24.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Impact of AI on Math“

Chu

Protect the social and ethical conditions of human mathematics, even at the cost of slower progress. Chu calls for a boycott of collaboration with AI companies and for not using LLMs to generate new proofs, although she considers complete worldwide abstention difficult to enforce.

  1. [14] Tasmin Chu. The AI dissenter viewpoint. 09.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Rebuttals...“; „Some radical proposals“

Stankyavichyus

Assess research by its benefit to others, including downstream sciences. In his view, those who reject available tools and primarily cite education as their contribution should be funded as teachers rather than as researchers.

  1. [54] Anatoly Vitold Stankyavichyus. Response to “The AI dissenter viewpoint”. 20.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „AI talking points“

Nigam

Technical progress does not create an obligation to accelerate as much as possible. Assess benefits alongside harms during the transition, listen to affected people and give researchers a say in the pace. Analogies cannot replace that assessment; she does not offer a complete transition programme.

  1. [45] Nilima Nigam. On analogies, parallels and thought experiments. 01.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „My concerns“; „In closing“

Litt

Understanding, theory-building and consolidation remain tasks. Different interests and respective strengths could enable a division of labour between humans and AI. Litt does not guarantee a lasting human performance advantage.

  1. [34] Daniel Litt. Mathematics in the Library of Babel. 21.02.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Creativity“; „The library“

Narayanan

AI models’ capabilities, genuinely usable products and organisational change are different things. Strengthen expertise, judgement and responsibility; rapid technical progress alone does not determine how professions change.

  1. [43] Arvind Narayanan. What will be left for us to work on? 13.07.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Vortragstranskript

Bessis

Reorient the goals and evaluation of mathematical work now. His expectation of a later reorganisation includes neither a timetable nor a secure transition strategy.

  1. [9] David Bessis. The fall of the theorem economy. 21.04.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Coming clean“; „The future of mathematics“

Castillo-Ramirez

Select important questions, check results, organise them and make connections; in teaching, convey enthusiasm, trust and perseverance. His anticipated change of roles does not guarantee lasting human superiority in these tasks.

  1. [12] Alonso Castillo-Ramirez. The end of an era in mathematical research. 12.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „The End of Handcrafted Mathematics“; „Teaching Will Remain Human“

Thurston

Understanding encompasses different mental approaches: images, language, intuition, logical structure and procedures. What matters is whether others can think more clearly; knowing a truth value or a formal derivation does not exhaust this.

  1. [65] William P. Thurston. On proof and progress in mathematics. 1994.Locator as given in the German collection: §§1–3

Tao

Tao proposes a publication threshold: the authors should be capable of presenting their results correctly, knowledgeably, intelligibly and with appropriate attribution. This is a capability criterion, not a general requirement to submit a talk.

  1. [59] Terence Tao. Mathematics in the age of AI. 2026; arXiv:2608.16753v1.Locator as given in the German collection: §8

Tao on the division of labour

Formal proof assistants allow subtasks of a formalisation project to be checked separately. Tao considers a similar division of labour possible for new results: individuals could make specialised contributions without fully understanding the entire project. Formalisation would need to become more efficient for this.

  1. [58] Terence Tao. Machine assisted proof. Manuskript 10.02.2024; Notices AMS, January 2025.Locator as given in the German collection: §1, S.4

Stankyavichyus: a contrasting position

Let novelty, correctness, impact and peer review suffice for publication; assess personal knowledge in hiring. He thus separates the value of a text from its authors’ understanding.

  1. [54] Anatoly Vitold Stankyavichyus. Response to “The AI dissenter viewpoint”. 20.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „AI talking points“

Shalit

Research has long depended on other people’s established theorems and distributed expertise. Personally tracing everything back to its foundations is untenable. AI intensifies this dependence; he gives no general new boundary for acceptable trust.

  1. [51] Orr Shalit. What’s not new. 08.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Standing on thin air“

Rivera: a stricter educational standard

Rivera requires his doctoral students to disclose AI use and strongly recommends limiting it. Statements used should be fully understood regardless of their origin; in supervision meetings, he asks questions down to fundamental principles. This is his personal supervision policy.

  1. [48] Manuel Rivera. Statement on AI usage for PhD students. 26.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Absätze über Offenlegung, Nutzungseinschränkung und Verständnis

B: Authorship, transparency and incentives

Etingof

Human authors can receive full credit, even when AI supplied proofs, provided they can personally check, understand, explain and take responsibility for the content, and identify the AI contributions.

  1. [18] Pavel Etingof, mit Beiträgen von Andrew Sutherland. Use of AI in mathematical research: A guide for young mathematicians. May 2026.Locator as given in the German collection: §1, S.1–2; §7, S.8

Weinreich

Try a model of recognition in which co-ownership of mathematical ideas can be extended later: anyone who subsequently demonstrates author-level understanding can join; journals should organise this demonstration.

  1. [70] Max Weinreich. The crisis of AI-generated mathematics. August 2026.Locator as given in the German collection: §2: „For journals“

Lazić

A prompt alone does not establish authorship. For him, doing one’s own theory-building or proof work is constitutive of mathematical research.

  1. [30] Vladimir Lazić. Where I stand on AI. undated; accessed 12.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Grundsatz 6

Thom

A correct AI result can merit publication without demonstrating human discovery or understanding. Credit that cannot be attributed should not automatically go to the model operator. Even with data protection settled, Thom considers the existing system of personal priority barely sustainable: publications no longer reliably demonstrate their authors’ contributions and abilities.

  1. [63] Andreas Thom. On the existence of non-sofic groups. 11.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Schlussteil

Thom

Account for the spectrum from a mere prompt to decisive mathematical collaboration. How that contribution can be reliably checked and attributed remains an open question.

  1. [64] Andreas Thom. Six questions on AI, mathematics and institutional arrangements. Provided in conversation.Locator as given in the German collection: Punkt 1Provided contribution · no public link in the collection

Leiden Declaration

Identify and acknowledge the underlying human work behind AI-generated results. If attribution remains inadequate despite efforts, explicitly disclose this.

  1. [33] Leiden Declaration working group. Leiden Declaration on Artificial Intelligence and Mathematics. 02.06.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Put effort into proper attribution“

Ochigame

Shift recognition from first publication towards incremental collective work, including programming, data work, formalisation and explanation; reward contributions more fairly.

  1. [46] Rodrigo Ochigame. Automated Mathematics and the Reconfiguration of Proof and Labor. 2023; Bulletin of the AMS 2024.Locator as given in the German collection: §5

Avigad

Using the formalisation of the sphere-packing theorem as an example, Avigad calls for appropriate recognition of prior human work and for bringing AI-generated code up to the project’s standards together with its team.

  1. [4] Jeremy Avigad. Mathematicians in the Age of AI. March 2026; version 06.04.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: §2

Hairer

Disclose which substantial mathematical ideas or proofs came from AI. Under Hairer’s proposal, routine language correction and literature searches need not be declared.

  1. [22] Martin Hairer. Writing mathematics in the age of AI. 07.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Disclosure“

LMS

For its journals, the London Mathematical Society requires disclosure of all AI use, including language assistance. Human authors bear full responsibility; generative AI may not be listed as an author.

  1. [36] London Mathematical Society. Artificial Intelligence. as of: June 2026.Locator as given in the German collection: §§2.1.5, 2.5

Klowden and Tao

Responsible use can be driven out both by reckless competition and by blanket exclusion. Good practice, education and targeted regulation should encourage helpful use and limit harmful or concealed use.

  1. [27] Tanya Klowden and Terence Tao. Mathematical methods and human thought in the age of AI. 27.03.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: §5.4

Stankyavichyus

Reject shaming and sanctions solely for using AI. Norms set by permanently employed researchers can impose particularly high career costs on their students and precariously employed colleagues.

  1. [54] Anatoly Vitold Stankyavichyus. Response to “The AI dissenter viewpoint”. 20.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „On dangers of dehumanizing colleagues“

Ruodu Wang

In Wang’s thought experiment, if the first solution receives most of the credit and unchecked adoption of AI output is undetectable, authors who check carefully are disadvantaged. Reducing priority rewards does not solve this while later work receives nothing. He leaves open a suitable replacement metric, especially for early-career researchers.

  1. [67] Ruodu Wang. The AI-assisted mathematician’s dilemma. 28.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Annahmen i–iii; Schlussabsätze

Boldt

Disclose the exact model version, prompts and orchestration software; even nondeterministic experiments remain open to scientific scrutiny and investigation.

  1. [10] Axel Boldt. Explanations, Prompts, and Formalizations: Arguments for New Norms in LLM-Enabled Mathematical Research. August 2026.Locator as given in the German collection: §5

Hairer

Available reasoning traces can help; he considers general requirements to provide prompts and consumption data impractical because of availability and lack of reproducibility.

  1. [22] Martin Hairer. Writing mathematics in the age of AI. 07.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Disclosure“

Gonen Cohen

Gonen Cohen discusses public access, access on request or access for trusted reviewers as possible options. Prompt and tool histories might offer a compromise; reasoning traces do not necessarily reliably reflect how an answer was actually produced.

  1. [20] Segev Gonen Cohen. The question of reasoning traces. 11.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „In what format...“; „What next?“

C: Correctness, reviewing and communication

Ringer

Separate generation from checking: a small kernel checks the formal proof object independently of the reliability of the system that generated it.

  1. [47] Talia Ringer. Proofs and Conversations. 17.05.2024.Locator as given in the German collection: „Proof Assistants“, S.2; „Choosing a Proof Assistant“, S.3

Etingof and Sutherland

Personally check, understand and explain arguments. For formalisation, also verify that the encoded theorem matches the intended statement; agreement among several models is not a proof.

  1. [18] Pavel Etingof, mit Beiträgen von Andrew Sutherland. Use of AI in mathematical research: A guide for young mathematicians. May 2026.Locator as given in the German collection: §1; §4.4; §4.6 (Andrew Sutherland)

Boldt

Those publishing mathematical results obtained through language models should provide a machine-checkable formalisation. This is Boldt’s proposed norm, not a generally applicable rule.

  1. [10] Axel Boldt. Explanations, Prompts, and Formalizations: Arguments for New Norms in LLM-Enabled Mathematical Research. August 2026.Locator as given in the German collection: §4

Klowden and Tao

Formalisation does not automatically check whether the encoded statement is the intended one. Motivation, heuristics and transferability remain additional contributions to knowledge.

  1. [27] Tanya Klowden and Terence Tao. Mathematical methods and human thought in the age of AI. 27.03.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: §4.4

Weinberger

Do not confuse convincing conversation with reliability. Maintain critical exchanges with colleagues and require formal evidence or detailed independent understanding; linguistic persuasiveness can shift one’s judgement unnoticed.

  1. [69] Shmuel Weinberger. What I feel like when I work with an AI. 11.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Haupttext

Lee

Distinguish three levels: proving the formal theorem from the axioms used, correctly translating the intended statement, and agreement with the original informal proof. A formally correct different proof does not automatically confirm the original argument.

  1. [32] Seewoo Lee.(auto)formalization, but why. 08.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Do you want to (auto)formalize a theorem or a proof?“

Leiden Declaration

Authors should facilitate checking through precise references and appropriate formal proofs; professional organisations could require additional human exposition or external pre-screening.

  1. [33] Leiden Declaration working group. Leiden Declaration on Artificial Intelligence and Mathematics. 02.06.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Support the needs of reviewing“; „Maintain standards of rigor“

Litt

Consider AI pre-screening, while accounting for erroneous decisions. He discusses deposits or restricting access to trusted authors as problematic barriers, not desirable ones.

  1. [34] Daniel Litt. Mathematics in the Library of Babel. 21.02.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Truth-seeking“

First Proof

In the AI evaluation project First Proof, independent expert reviewers assess the solutions. According to the report, unrestricted corporate donations fund, among other things, research grants for reviewers; the editorial panel and board are not remunerated from these funds.

  1. [1] Mohammed Abouzaid, Nikhil Srivastava, Rachel Ward and Lauren Williams. First Proof Second Batch. 10.06.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: §§2.3–2.4, S.10–11

Mangioni

He considers a possible model of AI-assisted generation with human checking and assimilation of the mathematics. Reviewing could become a regular work duty with allocated time. For AI applications overall, he calls for clear limits and rapidly adaptable corrective mechanisms.

  1. [39] Giorgio Mangioni. A viewpoint on “The AI dissenter viewpoint”. 14.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „The social context lens“; „The ethical lens“

Narayanan

Automate routine tasks while leaving evaluative decisions to people. Otherwise, researchers lose influence over the development of their field.

  1. [43] Arvind Narayanan. What will be left for us to work on? 13.07.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Schlussteil

LMS

For its journals, the London Mathematical Society prohibits AI reviewing and uploading manuscripts to AI systems. Reviewers may have a report they wrote themselves linguistically improved, but must disclose this and may transmit only the report text.

  1. [36] London Mathematical Society. Artificial Intelligence. as of: June 2026.Locator as given in the German collection: §§3.5, 4.6

Etingof (following a comment by Paul Seidel)

Those publishing must turn ideas into shared knowledge through explanations, conversations and talks; an arXiv upload alone is not enough.

  1. [18] Pavel Etingof, mit Beiträgen von Andrew Sutherland. Use of AI in mathematical research: A guide for young mathematicians. May 2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Bemerkung 5.1, S.8

Macbeth

Develop formal texts as a mathematical medium in their own right: organise recurring arguments using appropriate concepts and general auxiliary lemmas, and connect computation with abstraction.

  1. [38] Heather Macbeth. Algorithm and abstraction in formal mathematics. 07.05.2024.Locator as given in the German collection: §§2–4

Donoho

For data-based science, openly provide data, programs and computations that can be reproduced with little effort, and use shared benchmarking procedures. Creating missing datasets and benchmark tasks can itself be a significant research contribution.

  1. [16] David Donoho. Data Science at the Singularity. 02.10.2023.Locator as given in the German collection: §10.1

Tao

Formalisation can reveal unnecessary assumptions and simpler approaches and expand a reusable library; certification alone is only one possible purpose.

  1. [58] Terence Tao. Machine assisted proof. Manuskript 10.02.2024; Notices AMS, January 2025.Locator as given in the German collection: §1, S.3–4

Cheung

Explain to outsiders why a problem and its solution are interesting. Public communication can foster social support, one’s own understanding and better mathematical exposition; correctness alone does not convey significance.

  1. [13] Trevor Cheung. AI and maths communication. 03.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Lessons for academia?“; „A plea...“

Thom

A formal proof confirms a mathematical result; its significance, explanation and contextualisation require further work. For Thom, this process of making the mathematics one’s own is itself research.

  1. [63] Andreas Thom. On the existence of non-sofic groups. 11.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Schlussabsätze

D: Assessing capabilities and guiding research

First Proof

First Proof tests AI on research tasks whose human solutions already exist but remain unpublished. Solutions, logs, costs and orchestration code are disclosed; two to three expert reviewers assess them anonymously. This test does not capture concept development or finding interesting conjectures.

  1. [1] Mohammed Abouzaid, Nikhil Srivastava, Rachel Ward and Lauren Williams. First Proof Second Batch. 10.06.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: §§1.1–1.3, 2.1–2.4

Narayanan

Beyond success rates, assess whether AI works reliably on repeated attempts, is robust to small changes, correctly estimates its own errors and produces errors with manageable consequences.

  1. [43] Arvind Narayanan. What will be left for us to work on? 13.07.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Absätze zur Verlässlichkeit

Bessis

Develop a broader scale of automation that also covers simplification, formulating questions, and developing concepts and theories.

  1. [9] David Bessis. The fall of the theorem economy. 21.04.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Coming clean“

Lan

Systematically test publicly available models on open problems, independently check outputs and disclose use. The community should assess significance and performance itself; his further conjectures about companies’ motives are not evidence.

  1. [29] Tian Lan. On protecting mathematics from LLM companies’ monopoly. 18.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Absätze zu öffentlichen Tests und Begutachtung

Tao: diagnosis

Difficulty is relative to known methods: what is easy, achievable with effort or still out of reach? Tao sees this orientation threatened by rapid AI progress and AI companies’ failure to disclose unsuccessful attempts and solution processes. Competition for promising problems could encourage researchers to become secretive.

  1. [61] Terence Tao. Thread zur difficulty landscape. 08.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Teile 2–3

Tao: proposal

Develop shared standards for many classes of problems: a solution should also provide insights from the process that produced it and understanding of related difficulties. Without this analysis, a result may have little or even negative value for further research orientation.

  1. [61] Terence Tao. Thread zur difficulty landscape. 08.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Teil 4

Gonen Cohen

Discuss whether unsuccessful runs and their costs should also be disclosed, to what extent and to whom. He does not commit to a general publication requirement; reporting only successful traces can conceal the total effort.

  1. [20] Segev Gonen Cohen. The question of reasoning traces. 11.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „What next?“

Tao

Tao describes an interplay of machine pattern recognition, human conjecture formation, cross-checking and proof work. A case study in knot theory shows how this can produce new insights; it does not establish a general performance forecast.

  1. [58] Terence Tao. Machine assisted proof. Manuskript 10.02.2024; Notices AMS, January 2025.Locator as given in the German collection: §2, S.5–6

Guanyang Wang

Wang proposes a possible division of labour: humans supply a proof plan with open intermediate steps, and AI helps with those steps. He also reports successful and failed runs of two prompts. The prompt comparison, devised with knowledge of the proof, does not allow a general prediction of success.

  1. [66] Guanyang Wang. Prompting Toward a Conjecture. 12.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „The Right Prompt“; „A Hypothetical Example“

Hartstein

Research automated proving more broadly than language models, including symbolic and hybrid approaches. His goals are verifiable correctness, the ability in principle to find any proof that exists in the chosen formal system, and efficient search. He does not demonstrate that all these goals are generally achievable in practice.

  1. [23] Oren Hartstein. We Need a Stockfish for Math. 11.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „The Actual Goal“; „The Gödel Objection“; „The Future“

Avigad

Help develop formal libraries, symbolic methods, experiments and new forms of interaction, and recognise this as research. If mathematics merely recommends that other sciences use ready-made AI, it risks losing relevance. It should contribute its own methods and applications.

  1. [5] Jeremy Avigad. What is mathematics now, and what should it be? 07.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „What is AI for mathematics?“; „The challenges“; „The future“

Zhang

In his account of a group-theoretic construction, Zhang describes how the team stopped costly computational approaches, changed coordinates and strategy, and used AI for programming and analysis. The results were independently checked with mathematical software. More computing power alone would not have replaced the change of strategy.

  1. [72] Shaowu Zhang. Where to Point the Telescope. 21.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „When more precision was the wrong answer“; „What I learned“

E: Degree programmes, doctoral study and approaches to learning

Thom

Design programmes up to the doctorate around the requirements of the labour market, including academic careers. Problem-solving in collaboration with AI and making AI-generated results useful should be central.

  1. [64] Andreas Thom. Six questions on AI, mathematics and institutional arrangements. Provided in conversation.Locator as given in the German collection: Punkt 2Provided contribution · no public link in the collection

Avigad; Commelin et al.

Avigad calls for professionally relevant problem-solving with AI while preserving mathematical intuition. Commelin and co-authors recommend strengthening problem formulation, communication and critical scrutiny of arguments in the curriculum, and involving students in its design.

  1. [4] Jeremy Avigad. Mathematicians in the Age of AI. March 2026; version 06.04.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: §§3–4
  2. [15] Commelin, Jamnik, Ochigame, Taelman and Venkatesh. Shaping the Future of Mathematics in the Age of AI. 26.03.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: S.3

Su

Orient mathematical education also towards perseverance, creativity, changes of perspective and responsibility. Its value extends beyond practically useful solutions.

  1. [55] Francis Su. The Enduring Value of Math, in an Age of AI. 20.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „What a math education is really for“

Thom (teaching concept)

Develop teaching and assessment from the target competencies for research and employment: precise use of concepts, checking proofs, examples and counterexamples, recognising structures and mathematical responsibility.

  1. [62] Andreas Thom. A new teaching concept for mathematics programmes: Lecture – AI Tutor – Foundational Exercise Class – Laboratory. Undated; 5 pages.Locator as given in the German collection: S.1Provided contribution · no public link in the collection

MIT report

Start from learning goals and then permit, require or limit AI accordingly. Teach effective, responsible and ethical use in degree programmes and doctoral education.

  1. [42] MIT Ad Hoc Committee on AI Use in Teaching, Learning, and Research Training. Report on AI Use in Teaching, Learning, and Research Training. 13.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: §§2.5–2.7, 3.2.4

Thom (teaching concept)

Connect four elements: the lecture conveys guiding ideas and connections; a course-specific AI tutor supports self-study; the foundational exercise class trains independent mathematical thinking in person, largely without AI; the laboratory practises controlled use of AI, programming and other tools.

  1. [62] Andreas Thom. A new teaching concept for mathematics programmes: Lecture – AI Tutor – Foundational Exercise Class – Laboratory. Undated; 5 pages.Locator as given in the German collection: §§1.1–1.4, S.2–3Provided contribution · no public link in the collection

Thom (roles and workload)

The lecture notes remain the authoritative reference; AI explanations should be checked against them and against one’s own reasoning. An illustrative redistribution is two weekly semester hours each for lecture, foundational exercise class and laboratory instead of four for lectures plus two for exercises; the AI tutor supports learning outside contact hours.

  1. [62] Andreas Thom. A new teaching concept for mathematics programmes: Lecture – AI Tutor – Foundational Exercise Class – Laboratory. Undated; 5 pages.Locator as given in the German collection: §1 und §1.2, S.2Provided contribution · no public link in the collection

Thom (programme structure)

First secure shared foundations, then adjust emphasis by study pathway: for theory, for example, proof and literature seminars and formal verification; for applications, modelling, data analysis and simulation. Independent competence remains necessary in every pathway.

  1. [62] Andreas Thom. A new teaching concept for mathematics programmes: Lecture – AI Tutor – Foundational Exercise Class – Laboratory. Undated; 5 pages.Locator as given in the German collection: §2, S.3–4Provided contribution · no public link in the collection

Jaye and Livshyts

Try tasks on checking proofs, selecting and justifying interesting questions, writing intelligibly and giving short talks. These can be combined: work on one’s own questions, with AI where appropriate, write an understandable report and defend the results in class.

  1. [25] Benjamin Jaye, Galyna V. Livshyts. Changes in the classroom in the AI Era. 19.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: nummerierte Aufgabenfolge

Etingof

Attempt problems independently first, ask for hints rather than complete solutions and work through exercises oneself. AI should enhance learning.

  1. [18] Pavel Etingof, mit Beiträgen von Andrew Sutherland. Use of AI in mathematical research: A guide for young mathematicians. May 2026.Locator as given in the German collection: §§3, 4.5

Karamanis

Difficulties and debugging are part of developing the judgement that will later supervise AI. One must remain able to explain methods and decisions oneself.

  1. [26] Minas Karamanis. The machines are fine. I’m worried about us. 30.03.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Absätze zur wissenschaftlichen Betreuung

Siktar

Carefully work through any AI arguments used on paper. When correcting code, students should explain the cause of the error, the effect of the correction and suitable tests. He proposes joint prompting with supervisors as a teaching method he has not yet tried himself.

  1. [52] Joshua Siktar. How to welcome LLM use into student research mentorship. 09.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Be efficient...“; „Don’t autopilot debugging“; „Tag-team...“

Tao

Tedious calculation can be delegated if its results can be checked. Conceptual difficulties are often themselves the object of learning; even AI explanations can take away this learning opportunity. Provide supervised use, independent failed attempts and joint checking of AI outputs.

  1. [56] Terence Tao. How should university students control their AI diet? 10.06.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: S.19–25

Srivathsan

A tutor should build on the learner’s own attempt: ask questions to test understanding, give hints as needed, clarify prerequisites and offer small examples or simpler subproblems. Preserve productive failed attempts instead of immediately supplying the solution.

  1. [53] Amruth Srivathsan. Keeping the learner in the loop. 13.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Haupttext
