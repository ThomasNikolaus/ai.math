Here we collect important questions and different perspectives for the mathematical community.

The perspectives presented do not necessarily reflect the views of the authors of this site. The summaries may omit nuances; please consult the linked original sources for the precise positions.

A: Goals, progress and understanding

Avila et al.

Develop concepts and human understanding. Solutions should enter mathematical culture through careful exposition, simplification, conversation and teaching.

  1. [12] Avila et al. A Severe Misalignment of AI in Mathematics. undated; accessed 12.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Haupttext

Bessis

Credit for being the first to prove a theorem was an imperfect proxy for understanding. Recognition and public presentation should focus more on concepts, insight and clarification.

  1. [17] David Bessis. The fall of the theorem economy. 21.04.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Coming clean“

Kunnawalkam Elayavalli

Cultivate personal and shared moments of understanding, mathematical taste and cultural experience. Judge AI by whether it enriches that experience; a machine’s superiority does not, for him, determine the value of human mathematics.

  1. [61] Srivatsav Kunnawalkam Elayavalli. Why do mathematics in the age of AI. 09.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Haupttext

Wegner: a different account of value

Locate mathematics’ core value in applications, including through long chains of abstract theories. Wegner assumes reliable formal verification. As long as humans must set research directions and assess results, AI should open up previously unrealistic tasks and connect mathematical tools to scientific needs.

  1. [121] Robert Wegner. A somewhat optimistic view of AI in mathematics. 10.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Annahme A1; Szenario S1; These T2

Andrew Ng (Bonn)

Preserve human understanding also as a safeguard for scientific independence. His positive vision of AI-assisted explanation assumes ethically and ecologically acceptable conditions. Ng regards present conditions as unacceptable and therefore rejects generative language models.

  1. [82] Andrew Ng (mathematics doctoral student, Bonn). On proof and progress in mathematics in the age of generative LLMs. 04.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Why is human understanding important?“; „Back to reality. . . “

Bader

From a Platonist perspective, research explores an independently existing mathematical reality. AI can provide better tools for this, much as satellites do for cartography. Bader advocates embracing the new possibilities and adapting professional practice; beauty, understanding and socially fruitful connections to the physical world remain goals for him.

  1. [13] Uri Bader. The Platonist view. 13.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Haupttext

Gowers: objection to a single hierarchy of goals

Problem-solving and conceptual understanding can each serve as a means to the other. Different mathematical motivations are legitimate; in his view, simply declaring understanding to be the sole overriding goal fails to do justice to this diversity.

  1. [45] Timothy Gowers. Why I didn’t sign the Fields medallists’ letter. 17.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Absätze zum Spektrum zwischen problem solving und conceptual understanding

Marcolli

Understand mathematics as a language for making sense of the world and as a form of personal expression. On this view, a machine-generated solution does not deprive one's own mathematical work of meaning; researchers should also be able to pursue their own questions.

  1. [77] Matilde Marcolli. Duet for the End of Math. manuscript 10.09.2026; Newsfeed 13.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: S. 3–4

Tao

Separate predictions about capabilities from value judgements. Even assuming highly capable AI, insight, theory-building, education and the future of the community remain goals in their own right.

  1. [107] Terence Tao. Mathematics in the age of AI. 2026; arXiv:2608.16753v1.Locator as given in the German collection: §§4–5

Barak

He does not regard complete abstention from AI as viable for application-oriented academic mathematics. At the same time, he defends human curiosity and intelligible science as values in their own right; the applications of fundamental research cannot be measured over the short term.

  1. [14] Boaz Barak. Math after AI. 24.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Impact of AI on Math“

Chu

Protect the social and ethical conditions of human mathematics, even at the cost of slower progress. Chu calls for a boycott of collaboration with AI companies and for not using LLMs to generate new proofs, although she considers complete worldwide abstention difficult to enforce.

  1. [25] Tasmin Chu. The AI dissenter viewpoint. 09.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Rebuttals. . . “; „Some radical proposals“

Stankyavichyus

Assess research by its benefit to others, including downstream sciences. In his view, those who reject available tools and primarily cite education as their contribution should be funded as teachers rather than as researchers.

  1. [101] Anatoly Vitold Stankyavichyus. Response to “The AI dissenter viewpoint”. 20.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „AI talking points“

Nigam

Technological progress does not create an obligation to accelerate as much as possible. Assess benefits and transitional harms together, listen to those affected and let researchers help determine the pace. Analogies cannot replace this judgement.

  1. [84] Nilima Nigam. On analogies, parallels and thought experiments. 01.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „My concerns“; „In closing“

Litt

Understanding, theory-building and consolidation remain tasks. Different interests and respective strengths could enable a division of labour between humans and AI. Litt does not guarantee a lasting human performance advantage.

  1. [69] Daniel Litt. Mathematics in the Library of Babel. 21.02.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Creativity“; „The library“

Narayanan

AI models’ capabilities, genuinely usable products and organisational change are different things. Strengthen expertise, judgement and responsibility; rapid technical progress alone does not determine how professions change.

  1. [81] Arvind Narayanan. What will be left for us to work on? 13.07.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Vortragstranskript

Bessis

Reorient the goals and evaluation of mathematical work now. His expectation of a later reorganisation includes neither a timetable nor a secure transition strategy.

  1. [17] David Bessis. The fall of the theorem economy. 21.04.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Coming clean“; „The future of mathematics“

Castillo-Ramirez

Select important questions, check results, organise them and make connections; in teaching, convey enthusiasm, trust and perseverance. His anticipated change of roles does not guarantee lasting human superiority in these tasks.

  1. [21] Alonso Castillo-Ramirez. The end of an era in mathematical research. 12.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „The End of Handcrafted Mathematics“; „Teaching Will Remain Human“

Rivera

Distinguish mathematics as a body of ideas, as a vocation practised individually and collectively, and as an institutionally organised profession. Rivera expects changes above all in professional structures. He regards the pleasure of aesthetically guided understanding and the community of mathematical friendships as more enduring; he leaves open how AI should be used within them.

  1. [91] Manuel Rivera. On the impact of AI on the mathematical practice. 12.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Haupttext, Unterscheidung (1)–(3) und Schlussabsätze

Litt (future scenario)

Prepare institutions for the possibility that AI also becomes highly capable at explanation and theory-building. Human understanding nevertheless arises only through human learning. Seminars, supervision and thinking together should therefore remain goals in their own right; this approach should not depend on a lasting human advantage in performance.

  1. [68] Daniel Litt. A beginning for mathematics. 14.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Einleitung; „What are we even trying to do here?“; „Producing high-quality mathematics“, Schluss

Doğan

Do not assume that AI can generate proofs but cannot provide good explanations. Examples, connections to the literature and clear exposition could also improve. A future role for humans therefore cannot rest solely on an asserted permanent advantage in explanation.

  1. [83] Timothy Nguyen; M. Levent Doğan. Two responses to “A Severe Misalignment of AI in Mathematics”. 18.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Zweite unabhängige Stellungnahme, §§2.5–2.7

Loh

Make human well-being an explicit goal of research. According to Loh, for people to steer research directions and AI systems responsibly, they need their own expertise at the research frontier and must maintain it through active research.

  1. [71] Po-Shen Loh. Why Do We Need Human Mathematicians Anymore? 19.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Why we really need people to work“

Thurston

Understanding encompasses different mental approaches: images, language, intuition, logical structure and procedures. What matters is whether others can think more clearly; knowing a truth value or a formal derivation does not exhaust this.

  1. [116] William P. Thurston. On proof and progress in mathematics. 1994.Locator as given in the German collection: §§1–3

Tao

Tao proposes a publication threshold: the authors should be capable of presenting their results correctly, knowledgeably, intelligibly and with appropriate attribution. This is a capability criterion, not a general requirement to submit a talk.

  1. [107] Terence Tao. Mathematics in the age of AI. 2026; arXiv:2608.16753v1.Locator as given in the German collection: §8

Tao on the division of labour

Formal proof assistants allow subtasks of a formalisation project to be checked separately. Tao considers a similar division of labour possible for new results: individuals could make specialised contributions without fully understanding the entire project. Formalisation would need to become more efficient for this.

  1. [106] Terence Tao. Machine assisted proof. manuscript 10.02.2024; Notices AMS, January 2025.Locator as given in the German collection: §1, S.4

Stankyavichyus: a contrasting position

Let novelty, correctness, impact and peer review suffice for publication; assess personal knowledge in hiring. He thus separates the value of a text from its authors’ understanding.

  1. [101] Anatoly Vitold Stankyavichyus. Response to “The AI dissenter viewpoint”. 20.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „AI talking points“

Shalit

Research has long depended on other people’s established theorems and distributed expertise. Personally tracing everything back to its foundations is untenable. AI intensifies this dependence; he gives no general new boundary for acceptable trust.

  1. [97] Orr Shalit. What’s not new. 08.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Standing on thin air“

Rivera: a strict standard for doctoral training

Rivera requires his doctoral students to disclose their AI use and strongly recommends limiting it. Statements they use should be fully understood, whatever their source; in supervision discussions, he asks questions down to fundamental principles.

  1. [92] Manuel Rivera. Statement on AI usage for PhD students. 26.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Absätze über Offenlegung, Nutzungseinschränkung und Verständnis

Nguyen

For Nguyen, mathematical understanding is changing, distributed and selective; even human theories often exceed any individual's knowledge. It is therefore possible to build on formally verified AI results before every detail has been understood by humans. Deciding which parts should be explained and understood collectively remains a task of mathematical judgement.

  1. [83] Timothy Nguyen; M. Levent Doğan. Two responses to “A Severe Misalignment of AI in Mathematics”. 18.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Erste unabhängige Stellungnahme, Absätze 2–5

Antieau

Before publication, authors should be able to reproduce the main ideas themselves, write intelligibly for humans and take intellectual responsibility. He permits linguistic revision by AI but rejects a language model generating the first draft of a manuscript.

  1. [4] Ben Antieau. Fast math/slow math. 15.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Collective standards“, Punkt 3

Evans (motivation)

Do not present either the end of mathematics or their own redundancy to young researchers as inevitable. Evans sees a task for experienced colleagues in sustaining interest and scope for action, and in grounding the value of mathematical work in the advancement of human understanding.

  1. [38] Jonny Evans. Many choices lie ahead. 21.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Haupttext, Absätze 3 und 8–9

Guillen

New mathematical insights can be a source of shared pride even when individual researchers also feel a sense of loss. New professional and communal structures should enable as many people as possible to continue participating in mathematics.

  1. [47] Nestor Guillen. Happy, those able to know the causes of things. 13.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Schlussabsätze

Strogatz: personal account

His account shows that enthusiasm for new possibilities and wider access can coexist with grief over familiar ways of engaging with mathematics. He attributes his feelings to the abrupt transformation of a long human tradition and offers no simple resolution.

  1. [102] Steven Strogatz. Wimbledon, the U.S. Open, and the future of mathematics. 12.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Persönlicher Bericht, insbesondere Anfang und Schluss

Friedman: an interpretive approach

In the abstract, Friedman interprets uncertainty as a crisis of orientation concerning mathematicians' place in research increasingly shaped by AI. He connects this with opaque mathematical processes and changing roles. The approach is a psychoanalytically informed interpretation.

  1. [41] Michael Friedman. Mathematical Anxiety in the Age of AI: Opacity, Role Inversion, and the Changing Place of the Mathematician. 18.08.2026; abstract consulted.Locator as given in the German collection: Abstract

B: Authorship, transparency and incentives

Etingof

Human authors can receive full credit, even when AI supplied proofs, provided they can personally check, understand, explain and take responsibility for the content, and identify the AI contributions.

  1. [37] Pavel Etingof, with contributions from Andrew Sutherland. Use of AI in mathematical research: A guide for young mathematicians. May 2026.Locator as given in the German collection: §1, S.1–2; §7, S.8

Weinreich

Try a model of recognition in which co-ownership of mathematical ideas can be extended later: anyone who subsequently demonstrates author-level understanding can join; journals should organise this demonstration.

  1. [123] Max Weinreich. The crisis of AI-generated mathematics. August 2026.Locator as given in the German collection: §2: „For journals“

Lazić

A prompt alone does not establish authorship. For him, doing one’s own theory-building or proof work is constitutive of mathematical research.

  1. [63] Vladimir Lazić. Where I stand on AI. undated; accessed 12.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Grundsatz 6

Thom

A correct AI result can merit publication without demonstrating human discovery or understanding. Credit that cannot be attributed should not automatically go to the model operator. Even with data protection settled, Thom considers the existing system of personal priority barely sustainable: publications no longer reliably demonstrate their authors’ contributions and abilities.

  1. [114] Andreas Thom. On the existence of non-sofic groups. 11.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Schlussteil

Thom

Recognise the spectrum from a mere prompt to decisive mathematical involvement. How this contribution can be reliably assessed and attributed remains an open institutional question.

  1. [115] Andreas Thom. Six questions on AI, mathematics and institutional design. Provided in conversation.Locator as given in the German collection: Punkt 1Provided contribution · no public link in the collection

Valentini

Link originality and recognition also to independent interpretation: connecting, contextualising and organising already generated and verified results into a theory can constitute an original scholarly contribution.

  1. [118] Lisa Valentini. When inventing is not enough. 15.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Inventing and interpreting, originality and credit“; Schluss von „3. Publication“

Craven

Journals should require a substantial human mathematical contribution and reject fully AI-generated work. For Craven, checking and understanding alone do not establish authorship. Human-designed strategies executed by machines may, however, be admissible; editors should judge borderline cases.

  1. [30] David Craven. Where will all the papers go? 19.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Humans only beyond this point“, Absätze 1–4

Kra

Connect human authorship with intellectual responsibility: authors should be able to explain the mechanism of a proof and how they arrived at it, answer questions and distinguish new ideas from borrowed components. Merely disclosing AI use and checking correctness is not enough.

  1. [60] Bryna Kra. Deep theorems were scarce and difficult and so became an effective mechanism to identify deep thought. AI has broken this system. 13.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Absätze zu human authorship und Schlussforderungen

Tao: authorised summary

Tao proposes that priority should require a publicly accessible scholarly explanation, in addition to the preprint and, potentially in future, formalisation. The latest date among these components could be decisive. An explanatory blog post or a recorded talk could also count; an early announcement of a solution alone would not suffice.

  1. [111] Terence Tao. Terence Tao on AI in mathematics (and beyond). updated 17.09.2026; proposals from August 2026 considered here.Locator as given in the German collection: Teil V, „Generation, verification, digestion“; Vorschlag vom 06.08.2026

Leiden Declaration

Identify and acknowledge the underlying human work behind AI-generated results. If attribution remains inadequate despite efforts, explicitly disclose this.

  1. [66] Leiden Declaration working group. Leiden Declaration on Artificial Intelligence and Mathematics. 02.06.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Put effort into proper attribution“

Ochigame

Shift recognition from first publication towards incremental collective work, including programming, data work, formalisation and explanation; reward contributions more fairly.

  1. [85] Rodrigo Ochigame. Automated Mathematics and the Reconfiguration of Proof and Labor. 2023; Bulletin of the AMS 2024.Locator as given in the German collection: §5

Avigad

Using the formalisation of the sphere-packing theorem as an example, Avigad calls for appropriate recognition of prior human work and for bringing AI-generated code up to the project’s standards together with its team.

  1. [10] Jeremy Avigad. Mathematicians in the Age of AI. March 2026; version 06.04.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: §2

Trombetti (contribution roles)

Make generation, checking, correction, formalisation and clear re-exposition visible as distinct contributions. They need not receive the same kind of recognition. A shared platform could also document later contributions to a result and thereby acknowledge collective work more appropriately.

  1. [117] Marco Trombetti. ProofForum: Keeping AI-Generated Mathematics Human. 22.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „The humanization problem“; „A collective problem requires a collective response“

Guillen

Understand advances in AI also as the fruit of mathematical knowledge built collectively.

  1. [47] Nestor Guillen. Happy, those able to know the causes of things. 13.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Cultural and social technologies“; „Convex hulls of ideas“

Hairer

Disclose which substantial mathematical ideas or proofs came from AI. Under Hairer’s proposal, routine language correction and literature searches need not be declared.

  1. [50] Martin Hairer. Writing mathematics in the age of AI. 07.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Disclosure“

LMS

For its journals, the London Mathematical Society requires disclosure of all AI use, including language assistance. Human authors bear full responsibility; generative AI may not be listed as an author.

  1. [72] London Mathematical Society. Artificial Intelligence. as of June 2026.Locator as given in the German collection: §§2.1.5, 2.5

Klowden and Tao

Responsible use can be driven out both by reckless competition and by blanket exclusion. Good practice, education and targeted regulation should encourage helpful use and limit harmful or concealed use.

  1. [58] Tanya Klowden und Terence Tao. Mathematical methods and human thought in the age of AI. 27.03.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: §5.4

Stankyavichyus

Reject shaming and sanctions solely for using AI. Norms set by permanently employed researchers can impose particularly high career costs on their students and precariously employed colleagues.

  1. [101] Anatoly Vitold Stankyavichyus. Response to “The AI dissenter viewpoint”. 20.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „On dangers of dehumanizing colleagues“

Ruodu Wang

In Wang’s thought experiment, if the first solution receives most of the credit and unchecked adoption of AI output is undetectable, authors who check carefully are disadvantaged. Reducing priority rewards does not solve this while later work receives nothing. He leaves open a suitable replacement metric, especially for early-career researchers.

  1. [120] Ruodu Wang. The AI-assisted mathematician’s dilemma. 28.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Annahmen i–iii; Schlussabsätze

Boldt

Disclose the exact model version, prompts and orchestration software; even nondeterministic experiments remain open to scientific scrutiny and investigation.

  1. [18] Axel Boldt. Explanations, Prompts, and Formalizations: Arguments for New Norms in LLM-Enabled Mathematical Research. August 2026.Locator as given in the German collection: §5

Hairer

Available reasoning traces can help; he considers general requirements to provide prompts and consumption data impractical because of availability and lack of reproducibility.

  1. [50] Martin Hairer. Writing mathematics in the age of AI. 07.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Disclosure“

Gonen Cohen

Gonen Cohen discusses public access, access on request or access for trusted reviewers as possible options. Prompt and tool histories might offer a compromise; reasoning traces do not necessarily reliably reflect how an answer was actually produced.

  1. [44] Segev Gonen Cohen. The question of reasoning traces. 11.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „In what format. . . “; „What next?“

Trombetti (ProofForum)

Experiment with a public, free repository in which AI-generated proof attempts are collectively checked, corrected and linked to the literature. It should remain clear what has merely been generated, what has been checked, what has failed and what remains uncertain. Unverified claims can also be discussed there; Trombetti explicitly describes ProofForum as an experiment.

  1. [117] Marco Trombetti. ProofForum: Keeping AI-Generated Mathematics Human. 22.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Generation is not verification“; „We may soon generate more mathematics than we can read“; „An experiment“

Auffinger

Change incentives and attribution so that sharing early remains a reasonable choice for young researchers too. He cites agreements on rapid data release and recognition in genomics as an inspiration. He explicitly acknowledges that he does not yet know a suitable mathematical counterpart; supervisors and young researchers should develop one together.

  1. [9] Antonio Auffinger. Open problems, open mathematics. 22.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Absätze zu Offenheit, Bermuda Principles und Fort Lauderdale Agreement

Cheung

Establish a register for drafts, partial calculations and notes with verifiable timestamps. Initially restrict access, then expand it gradually until public release. Later assessment should also consider contributions from unfinished ideas; a timestamp certifies their existence, not their significance. The procedure should rest on shared norms, without central permission to publish.

  1. [24] Kolen Cheung. Progressive disclosure for mathematical proofs. 12.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „The proposal: attestation, then widening circles“; „How do we credit unfinished work?“; „Who would enforce this?“

doomslide: an unresolved conflict

Even effective protection of private inputs does not eliminate the advantage of well-funded actors: unfinished ideas shared publicly could be developed more quickly with substantial computing resources. The author sees this as an incentive for secrecy and a threat to open exchange.

  1. [33] doomslide (pseudonym). Mathematics is effectively dead. 17.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: What can be done?, letzte Absätze

C: Correctness, reviewing and communication

Ringer

Separate generation from checking: a small kernel checks the formal proof object independently of the reliability of the system that generated it.

  1. [90] Talia Ringer. Proofs and Conversations. 17.05.2024.Locator as given in the German collection: „Proof Assistants“, S.2; „Choosing a Proof Assistant“, S.3

Etingof and Sutherland

Personally check, understand and explain arguments. For formalisation, also verify that the encoded theorem matches the intended statement; agreement among several models is not a proof.

  1. [37] Pavel Etingof, with contributions from Andrew Sutherland. Use of AI in mathematical research: A guide for young mathematicians. May 2026.Locator as given in the German collection: §1; §4.4; §4.6 (Andrew Sutherland)

Boldt

Anyone publishing mathematical results obtained through language models should provide a machine-checkable formalisation.

  1. [18] Axel Boldt. Explanations, Prompts, and Formalizations: Arguments for New Norms in LLM-Enabled Mathematical Research. August 2026.Locator as given in the German collection: §4

Klowden and Tao

Formalisation does not automatically check whether the encoded statement is the intended one. Motivation, heuristics and transferability remain additional contributions to knowledge.

  1. [58] Tanya Klowden und Terence Tao. Mathematical methods and human thought in the age of AI. 27.03.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: §4.4

Weinberger

Do not confuse convincing conversation with reliability. Maintain critical exchanges with colleagues and require formal evidence or detailed independent understanding; linguistic persuasiveness can shift one’s judgement unnoticed.

  1. [122] Shmuel Weinberger. What I feel like when I work with an AI. 11.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Haupttext

Lee

Distinguish three levels: proving the formal theorem from the axioms used, correctly translating the intended statement, and agreement with the original informal proof. A formally correct different proof does not automatically confirm the original argument.

  1. [65] Seewoo Lee. (auto)formalization, but why. 08.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Do you want to (auto)formalize a theorem or a proof?“

Lean FRO and SAIR Foundation

Improve the shared infrastructure for formal verification through more efficient algorithms and representations. For specified computational tasks, the Lean Kernel Challenge also requires a proof that the algorithm developed satisfies the specification for every input; speed alone is not enough.

  1. [108] Terence Tao; Lean FRO und SAIR Foundation. SAIR competition – Lean Kernel Challenge. 16.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Beschreibung von Stage 1 und Einreichungsanforderung

Muñoz-Lahoz

Plan and supervise AI-assisted formalisation closely so that libraries remain organised, understandable and reusable. Alongside machine checking of proofs, verify that the formal statements express the intended results. Work on a trustworthy shared foundation reduces repeated checking of the same definitions.

  1. [80] David Muñoz-Lahoz. The Banach lattice Lean library. 07.08.2026; version 20.08.2026, arXiv:2608.07388v2.Locator as given in the German collection: §1, Semiautoformalization

Leiden Declaration

Authors should facilitate checking through precise references and appropriate formal proofs; professional organisations could require additional human exposition or external pre-screening.

  1. [66] Leiden Declaration working group. Leiden Declaration on Artificial Intelligence and Mathematics. 02.06.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Support the needs of reviewing“; „Maintain standards of rigor“

Litt

Consider AI pre-screening, while accounting for erroneous decisions. He discusses deposits or restricting access to trusted authors as problematic barriers, not desirable ones.

  1. [69] Daniel Litt. Mathematics in the Library of Babel. 21.02.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Truth-seeking“

First Proof

In the AI testing project First Proof, independent expert reviewers assess the solutions. According to the report, unrestricted corporate donations fund, among other things, research grants for the reviewers.

  1. [1] Mohammed Abouzaid, Nikhil Srivastava, Rachel Ward und Lauren Williams. First Proof Second Batch. 10.06.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: §§2.3–2.4, S.10–11

Mangioni

He considers a possible model of AI-assisted generation with human checking and assimilation of the mathematics. Reviewing could become a regular work duty with allocated time. For AI applications overall, he calls for clear limits and rapidly adaptable corrective mechanisms.

  1. [75] Giorgio Mangioni. A viewpoint on “The AI dissenter viewpoint”. 14.08.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „The social context lens“; „The ethical lens“

Valentini (journals)

Use formal certificates to reduce the burden of reviewing; humans continue to check correspondence with the stated theorem, understanding, context and exposition. Journals could favour broader projects; she proposes separate repositories or journals for individual results largely generated by machines.

  1. [118] Lisa Valentini. When inventing is not enough. 15.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „3. Publication“

Craven (submission limits)

Consider temporary submission restrictions per author. Acceptance could lift a restriction; substantial deficiencies in the content could extend it. Craven aims to protect scarce reviewing capacity from excessive and inadequately checked output.

  1. [30] David Craven. Where will all the papers go? 19.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Humans only beyond this point“, Schlussabsätze

Cohn

Poorly explained proofs, even if correct, can create additional work for others to make sense of them. Those publishing such results should reduce this burden themselves: contact experts, explain how the work developed and use available AI or computing access to clarify it. Traditional reviewing cannot absorb arbitrarily large amounts of unprocessed output.

  1. [26] Henry Cohn. The technical debt of AI-generated mathematics. 15.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Open problems and AI“; „Technical debt“, Schlussabsätze

Gowers: a different assessment of abundant results

More results can enable both more unprocessed and more understood mathematics. Researchers could choose what to explore in depth; databases or AI-assisted overviews could help them navigate. He sees the greater danger in losing the social structures that sustain this work of understanding.

  1. [45] Timothy Gowers. Why I didn’t sign the Fields medallists’ letter. 17.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: Absätze zu collective understanding, Datenbanken und institutionellen Risiken

Cheung

AI laboratories should help fund clear exposition and the incorporation of reusable formal proof components into shared libraries. Formal verification, explanation and library maintenance are distinct tasks and can take place at different times.

  1. [24] Kolen Cheung. Progressive disclosure for mathematical proofs. 12.09.2026.Locator as given in the German collection: „Lean, mathlib, and who pays“

Tao: authorised summary

Those claiming credit for generating a proof should make their best effort to explain it and prepare it for publication. When this is not possible, Tao proposes a regulated procedure allowing a proof that has not yet been sufficiently worked through to be explicitly handed over to other authors for further development. Details and rules of recognition remain open.
